## Story time

My family was poor, so I never got pocket money. I never got money as gifts, so all I possessed were specific items from other people, usually my single-parent mom. I could ask for things, but ultimately she was the one deciding if we got something or not. So the first time I ever had money of my own was a big change for me.

It was a one-time scholarship, an end-of-the-school-year reward for excellent grades (I believe I had the best grades in my entire school). It wasn't A LOT, it couldn't solve my life, but it was more money than I ever had before.

I was excited to spend it. I knew I ha to spend it well, because it would be a long time before I got my hands on cash again. But I was a teenager, a middle-schooler, who lived in a tiny apartment with four other people and three cats, and exclusively wore hand-me-downs. I didn't even have my own corner in the room or a desk, everything shared with my older brother. I desperately wanted to spend the money on something _me_, on something that would express myself and/or make a tiny space marked as _mine_.

And then I saw it. A huge, elaborate desk lamp in the shape of a dragon. For a kid that only a year later finished reading the entirety of "Lord of the Rings", including the appendixes and dictionaries and glossaries, and was getting into tabletop games and fantasy genre… that was the coolest thing I have ever seen.

But it cost about ¾ of my cash. I had to think very, very carefully about if I really want to buy it or not. That's when the three questions I use to help me against impulsive purchases took form.

## Question 1: Why the fuck do I need it?

I asked my mom to help me decide, since I couldn't do it on my own. Her first doubt was why I really needed a lamp. To her, it was just a lamp (we were a Big Ceiling Light household and never used table/floor lamps) that was fancy-looking for no reason, and with a ridiculously high price-tag. So her first instinct was to question why I even need it?

Not want, need. What am I gonna use it for? Am I ever gonna use it? It sure looks neat, but like. If it's just going to be shelf decor, the price is ever more insane. 

And she was right. I was enamored by the coolness factor, but I did not need a lamp. 

## Question 2: Where the fuck am I gonna put it?

And speaking of shelves — shelf decor is great, she argued, but this thing is massive, especially for a lamp. Where are you going to put it? she asked me.

And she was right. We had to store a lot of stuff in a very small living space, so display shelves were a luxury we didn't have. Open shelves were small and designated for usually for frequently used items. I didn't have the shelf space for something like this. I could put it away into a cupboard or something, but then what would be the point of owning it?

I still don't live in big spaces, because my country mostly offers smaller dwellings, so I still think a lot about what I own and where I'll put it. How it will affect how I live, since I like my living space not cluttered. And, to be honest, I wouldn't want to live in a much bigger place anyway, because that is just so much more space to clean.

Which leads to the last question.

## Question 3: Who the fuck is gonna clean it?

The lamp was pretty detailed. It had scales of the dragon molded, and its claws, and some kind of rock-looking base on which the dragon was sitting, curled around a salt-crystal-looking source of light.

It had a lot of tiny crevices, is what I'm saying. 

And my mom was honestly obsessed with cleanliness (that's a topic a therapy session, not online journaling, though). So I knew I would have to clean the thing back to original state every so often, just like we did our yearly washing of all the crystal goblets we kept for special occasions and barely used. Or at least would have to dust it weekly, like everything else on the open shelves.

I did not want to dust the bumpy scales of a dragon lamp every week. 

## Summary

I did not buy the lamp. And now, even if I can easily afford most things these days, I don't accumulate cool Temu junk either. Because every time I see another item like that dragon lamp — something cool that would be great to own and I really like it — I sit down and ask myself these three questions before I even consider buying it. 

And then I sleep on it for a week or two.

And only then, when I really think I have a use for it, and the space for it, and it wouldn't be a nightmare to maintain, I buy it. 

It applies to appliances, decor, clothing, accessories. Even games and music, where the "storage space" isn't as limited and specific. And it saves me a lot of… well, money, yes, but mostly mental stress and overwhelm.

Shared it, so you might have that peace, too.
