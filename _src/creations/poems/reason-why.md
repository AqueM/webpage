I’ve always felt small
like my body was way bigger than my soul
like - even if I wasn’t size 16 - 
there will always be much more of me than there’s supposed to be.

And part of the reason is 
I learned - not ‘was taught’ because
who in their right mind
teaches a child that they don’t deserve the space they occupy
that they’re a waste of sustence they need to survive, to exist, 
because ‘to live’ is not something I did when I was a child.
Who in their right mind teaches a child to
east fast, hang your head low,
sulk in the corner, stay quiet
yell when you need to be heard because nobody ever listens
take bullying with a poker face because that’s just how kids are
always play alone because a smart person is never bored in their own company
never show anybody what you’ve made because nobody cares.

No, nobody teaches that 
but boy, do children learn fast.

Keep your drawings in the drawer
keep your poems short, words short, thoughts short
never sing, always do what you’re told. 
And when you realise you’re taller than them all
wider than them all
- weep, because you being big is something that makes you afraid
of all the things you’ll have to give up to fit the form.
And when you weep, weep quietly because
you’ve learned that the care is pretended 
and your problems
will never be big enough to matter.
You are supposed to be small
obedient, well-behaved, polite, helpful
grateful
“You can read books and have more than two dresses
your mother doesn’t beat you for disagreeing with religion”
so be small, be small, transparent, invisible
don’t get in my way, don’t argue, don’t worry,
we’re okay just don’t get in the way
you’ll be happy if you just stay small,
stay low.

Part of the reason why
I’d faster think of myself as a follower rather than a leader
a student, rather than an expert
a failure, rather than almost-there 
why people get angry at my loud voice, brave poses and bold gestures
and can’t understand how I can act so big and feel so small
Part of the reason why is
it’s the children who choose what you reinforce
and I only was safe when I was small, 
but I only mattered when I was big.
