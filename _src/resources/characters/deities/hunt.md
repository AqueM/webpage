- **Patronage:** people who were wronged or used, especially children
- **Symbols:** blood, feathers, teeth
- **Favoured animals:** wolves, working dogs, birds of prey

{% heading "h2", "Summary" %}

The Hunt is one being made out of three: **the Wolf**, **the Falcon** and **the Hound**. This is not a detail they divulge easily, and most of their followers think of those as just *aspects* of the Hunt, but they are in fact still somewhat separate. 

The details of how the Hunt came to be are shrouded in mystery, even though they're a relatively new player among the deities and powerful spirits. Fellow Archfey seem to think they used to be a triad of mortal lovers who somehow killed an Archfey, and afterwards took up the power together. 

As a "young" entity, they interact with the mortal world carefully and somewhat clumsily, and with other spirits almost not at all. They keep to themselves, prefer to lay in wait, and spring to action only when they deem the time right.

{% heading "h2", "Attributes" %}

### Morals and values

The Hunt cares about two things only: people wronged by others, and devotion. As such, they are fiercly territorial of those they deem to be *theirs*, stopping from *nothing* to protect them and help them get what they seek — especially if it's revenge. This makes them a very morally... dubious entity, especially with their penchant for blood, and little doubts about using violence. It is not uncommon for the Wolf to be involved in a murder, should someone endanger their pack, but it is just as likely for the Falcon to provide humanitarian aid in times of strife.

### Appearance

The different aspects appear accordingly to their names, as giant wolf, bird of prey or hound. They also tend to appear with specific attributes: the Wolf with bloodied fangs and claws, sometimes winged; the Falcon with brown-beige feathers, usually always moving and just outside the field of view; the Hound with white fur, sometimes stained with blood, and bright, green eyes. 

### Personality

Because to talk to all three of them at once is unheard of, how others perceive the Hunt depends entirely on which aspect they meet. The Wolf has a terrible reputation of being volatile and rash, the Falcon comes off as laid-back and relatively friendly, while the Hound seems to be the most talkative and rather helpful, but also very overbearing.

### Relations

The Hunt interacts with nobody but themselves and their protégés. They stay away from other spirits, even — or perhaps *especially* — from other Archfey. It makes them an unknown variable in the higher powers' politics; as neither an ally nor an enemy of anyone in particular, they are suspectible to be prey for everyone, and perhaps that is why they take great care to be secretive.

One thing can be ascertained, though: because of their *modus operandi* — functioning outside the law, aiding those who were forgotten or wronged by it — it is very unlikely any law-aligned deity would particularly like the Hunt. 

{% heading "h2", "Religion" %}

### Followers

There are very few followers of the Hunt, and usually they had not chosen the path willingly. Most of them sought power, for one reason or another, and bound themselves to the Hunt in exchange for it. Now, they do their patron's bidding and hunt for them among mortals — whatever hunting may mean at the time — allowing the Hunt to reach further and help more downtrodden people.

### Temples and priests

There are none, not even a shrine, and if one was built, it would be swiftly wrecked. The Hunt refuses to be classified as a *deity* — associating that term with being removed from the problems of mortals, as opposed to their direct involvement.

### Portrayal

There's no statue or portrait of them, and should one be made, it would certainly soon be destroyed. The Hunt likes their privacy, and cares not for vain devotionals.

### Rituals

There are no rituals that the Hunt demands or recognizes. Most followers figure out a personal way to commune with the Triumvirate and due to the innate association, it tends to involve drawing or sacrificing blood.
